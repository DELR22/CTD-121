Section 01

Introduction

In Assignment 06, we will learn by going backward. Using the same floor plan from Assignment 05, students must peel back the design layers to uncover the hidden spatial logic beneath.

Bloom’s Taxonomy (2001)

  1. Understand how the floor plan distributes area across functional zones by abstracting it into a Block Plan.
  2. Analyze spatial relationships between rooms and spaces by evaluating the type, quality, and logic of adjacencies encoded in the plan.
  3. Evaluate the designer’s spatial decisions by interpreting the Bubble Diagram and Matrix as evidence.
  4. Create a freestanding Synthesis Object that sequences the design process from abstract to concrete in a coherent volumetric display.

CIDA / NASAD Standards

  • Design Process: Demonstrate the ability to apply design process methodologies, including precedent analysis and spatial diagramming, to understand built environments.
  • Interior Building Systems & Integration: Identify and interpret the spatial organization and functional logic of an existing floor plan.
  • Building & Space Analysis: Analyze the functional relationships between spaces through graphic abstraction tools, including block plans, bubble diagrams, and adjacency matrices.
  • Communication: Communicate spatial analysis through disciplined hand drawing, graphic notation, and the physical assembly of a display object.

Section 02

The Deconstruction of the Floor Plan

Using the 1/8″ scale model from Assignment 05, students must create a horizontal slice by cutting through the walls at a height of 4 ft.

  1. Measure, using the 1/8″ = 1′-0″ scale, 4 feet from the base of the walls.
  2. Make several marks around the model, all at 4 feet.
    • Take photographs of this process.
  3. Trace a line that joins all 4′ markings around the model.
    • Take photographs of this process.
  4. Take a box cutter. Do not use a precision X-Acto knife. For this task, you will need a longer blade.
    • Make sure your box cutter has a new blade.
  5. Patience is key. Take the time you need to cut the horizontal section.
    • It is safer to do repetitive cuts than to try to cut everything with a single cut.
  6. When the slicing is done, remove the top part of the model.
    • Take photographs of this process.
  7. Color the sliced edges of the cut walls with a marker, in the color of your choosing, highlighting the horizontal section.
    • Take photographs of this process.
  8. Use the Scan function in the Notes app and create:
    • A crisp top photograph of the sliced model.
    • A crisp angled photograph of the sliced model.
      • It is very important that these photographs are clear and clean, without external shadows or objects in the shots.

What the Slice Shows

This exercise will show you what a floor plan means: a scaled, top-down, two-dimensional technical drawing that shows the spatial layout of a construction from a bird’s-eye view of a horizontal section.

What Assignment 05 drew is an abstraction of space. The sectioning is the experience of that abstraction.

Section 03

The Block Plan

Before you begin, use your 1/8″ scale model again to think about the activities that take place in each space. Not just their boundaries, but the life that occurs inside the edges of each room, of each space.

  1. Print the 1/8″ = 1′-0″ floor plan on an 8.5″ × 11″ format.
    • Download the format from this link: alabama.box.com/s/v3ghe2vr1vdqtwa60wtivpys6x2cc8e9
  2. Paste tracing paper on top of the entire floor plan.
  3. Trace the boundaries of every space. Remember that boundaries can be physical (a wall) or abstract (an open area divided by furniture). The result is a series of side-by-side boxes.
    • Using colors for different types of areas is recommended.
  4. For every major space, place a Room Tag inside its box. Small spaces such as closets and pantries should be included as separate boxes but are not required to be tagged.
  5. Each Room Tag must contain:
    NAME OF THE SPACEKITCHEN
    DIMENSIONS in Feet12′ × 8′
    AREA in Square Feet96 Sq. Ft.
  6. Measure the length and width of each major space from the 1/8″ drawings in the 1/8″ printout, calculate the area in square feet, name the space, and record all three in the tag.
  7. Take photographs of this process.
  8. Paste the printout with the tracing paper containing the Block Plan onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.

Section 04

The Bubble Diagram

  1. Use an 8.5″ × 11″ sheet of tracing paper.
  2. Following the instructions given in class, draw loose circles (bubbles) representing each major box on the Block Plan, using the same names and colors. Connect bubbles with the line types below to describe the relationships you observe between spaces in the plan.

Connection Line Types

Solid + ArrowPhysical connection—door or archway.
Dashed LineVisual connection—open plan, pass-through, sightline.
Zig-Zag LineSound barrier—acoustic separation between quiet and noisy zones.
Thick Dashed LineCirculation—hallways, corridors.
When finished, paste the tracing paper onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.

Section 04 · Continued

The Adjacency Matrix

  1. Download the Adjacency Matrix format from: alabama.box.com/s/tjgm4jcffzpvkthnb5j5fcudn931x61l
  2. Print the Adjacency Matrix in an 8.5″ × 11″ format.
  3. Follow the instructions given in class to complete the Adjacency Matrix.
    • For each space, consider and list the activities that occur within it.
    • Based on those activities, analyze the type of adjacency relationship between each of the spaces.
    • Fill every intersecting cell of the Adjacency Matrix grid using the three-symbol key below. Every cell must be filled. No blanks. Your symbol choices should be consistent with the Bubble Diagram and with what you observe in the floor plan.
    • For further guidance on completing the Adjacency Matrix, watch the explanation video: youtu.be/fYFwns2cZ30
  4. Paste the completed Adjacency Matrix onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.

Symbol Key

●Primary Adjacency—these spaces must be next to each other.
○Secondary Adjacency—these spaces should be close for convenience.
–No Relation—proximity does not matter.

Section 05

Client Profile & Design Concept Statement

Client Profile

  1. Download the Client Profile format from: alabama.box.com/s/jh7mwrtktuuu79kwff1c9grzc2dnifpz
  2. Select a client profile from the options given in the format.
  3. Search for images of people who fit that profile. Do not use AI to generate the images. For this, you may:
    • Use photos of your friends and family, including yourself.
    • Use magazines.
    • Search on the internet.
  4. Follow the instructions on the format to create the client backstory and the client representation collage.
    • You can do this manually or digitally.
    • At the end, complete and print the format, regardless of the medium.
  5. Paste the completed format onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.

Design Concept Statement

  1. Download the Design Concept Statement format from: alabama.box.com/s/jh7mwrtktuuu79kwff1c9grzc2dnifpz
  2. According to your client’s backstory, write a concept statement broken down into three paragraphs.
    • Paragraph 1: aspirational, conceptual, deep, and perceptual issues, such as feelings, atmospheric sensations, needs, and desires.
    • Paragraph 2: descriptions of the activities that take place in the main spaces of the house.
    • Paragraph 3: tangible spatial design decisions: spatial configuration, materials, colors, textures, finishes, and lighting design (natural and artificial).
    • Since you are deconstructing an existing house, your activity and spatial descriptions must match the spatial configuration of the floor plan and models you drew in Assignment 05.

Section 06

The Synthesis Object

Objective: Create a narrative within a volumetric composition that reconstructs and explains the design process.

Structure Requirements

Stand independently—the structure may not lean against a wall or another object.

Read in sequence—panels must be arranged so a viewer can follow the design process from most abstract to most concrete.

The Synthesis Object is not decoration. It is an argument: the panel sequence should tell the story of how a designer moves from schematic drawings and data to a fully realized floor plan and physical model.

Required Sequence — Abstract → Concrete

1
Adjacency Matrix
Most abstract — pure spatial data encoded in a grid
2
Bubble Diagram
Spatial relationships as circles and connection lines
3
Block Plan
Area distribution as labeled and colored zone boxes
4
Sliced 1/8″ Physical Model
Most concrete — built form in three-dimensional space

Instructions

  1. Connect, in a unique and creative way, all 8.5″ × 11″ foam boards, including the 1/8″ scale sliced model.
  2. Use masking tape to create hinges so they fold into a freestanding volumetric display structure.

Section 07

Evaluation Rubric

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
Sliced 1/8″ Model — Precision & Documentation 30 pts
26–30 pts
The model is sliced cleanly and level at 4 ft (measured with the 1/8″ = 1′-0″ scale) all the way around. The cut wall edges are colored precisely, clearly highlighting the horizontal section. Process photographs document every stage, and the top and angled scans are crisp, clean, and free of external shadows or objects.
16–25 pts
The slice is mostly level at 4 ft, with minor irregularities or rough edges. The cut edges are colored, but unevenly in places. Process photographs are present but miss some stages, or the scans show minor shadows or clutter.
0–15 pts
The slice is uneven, at the wrong height, or incomplete, with torn or crushed walls. The cut edges are not colored. Process photographs or the top and angled scans are missing, blurry, or cluttered.
Spatial Abstraction — Block Plan & Bubble Diagram 50 pts
44–50 pts
The block plan accurately captures the spatial distribution of the floor plan, with major spaces clearly defined, labeled, and tagged. The color system is consistent and carries legibly into the bubble diagram. Spatial relationships are correctly identified and meaningfully encoded using all four connection line types.
28–43 pts
The spatial distribution is mostly legible, but some spaces are imprecisely defined or incompletely tagged. The color system is present but inconsistent. The bubble diagram shows relationships, but the line types are misapplied.
0–27 pts
The spatial distribution does not clearly correspond to the floor plan. Tags are largely absent or incorrect. Color is missing or applied arbitrarily. The bubble diagram is incomplete or contradicts the block plan.
Adjacency Matrix — Completeness & Logic 30 pts
26–30 pts
All three symbols are used and applied consistently with the Bubble Diagram and the observed floor plan. Relationship assignments are spatially logical. No cells are left blank.
16–25 pts
At least two symbol types are used. Some relationships are logical, but others contradict the Bubble Diagram or lack spatial justification. A few cells are blank or carry the wrong symbol.
0–15 pts
The grid is incomplete. Only one symbol type is used. Relationships do not correspond to the Bubble Diagram or the observed floor plan.
Client Profile, Concept Statement & Material Board 40 pts
35–40 pts
The client backstory and collage follow the format and use real, non-AI images that fit the selected profile. The concept statement has three clear paragraphs (aspirations, activities, design decisions) that match the floor plan and models from Assignment 05. Both material boards (bedrooms and common areas) include the DREAMS-4-MatBoard process screenshots and AI images made with Empathic Prompting. All formats are printed and pasted onto foam board.
22–34 pts
The formats are complete, but the collage only partly fits the profile, one paragraph of the concept statement is weak or misses a required topic, or some activities do not match the floor plan. One material board or some process screenshots are missing.
0–21 pts
The formats are incomplete or not printed. The client collage uses AI-generated images. The concept statement lacks the three-paragraph structure or contradicts the floor plan. Material boards are missing or have no process screenshots.
Synthesis Object — Sequence & Craft 50 pts
44–50 pts
The sequence reads clearly from the adjacency matrix to the physical model. Panels are clean, flat, and presented with care. The object functions as a coherent argument about the design process.
28–43 pts
The structure stands but is unstable or requires minor support. The sequence is mostly legible, but one panel is out of order, or the transition between abstract and concrete is unclear. Some panels show handling damage or careless assembly.
0–27 pts
The structure does not stand independently. Panels are connected incorrectly or in an incoherent order. The sequence does not communicate the design process. Assembly is careless or incomplete.
Total 200 pts 170 pts max 105 pts max

Deliverables

Deliverable 01 — Physical Model
The Sliced 1/8″ Model
The 1/8″ scale model from Assignment 05, sliced horizontally through the walls at 4 ft (measured with the 1/8″ = 1′-0″ scale), with the top part removed and the cut wall edges colored with a marker to highlight the horizontal section. Include the process photographs and two crisp scans made with the Notes app: one top view and one angled view, clean and free of external shadows or objects.
Deliverable 02 — Foam Board
The Block Plan
The 1/8″ floor plan printout (8.5″ × 11″) with the tracing paper Block Plan on top: the boundaries of every space traced as side-by-side boxes, and a Room Tag (name, dimensions in feet, area in square feet) in every major space. Pasted onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.
Deliverable 03 — Foam Board
The Bubble Diagram
An 8.5″ × 11″ tracing paper sheet with loose bubbles for each major space of the Block Plan, using the same names and colors, connected with the four line types (physical, visual, sound barrier, circulation). Pasted onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.
Deliverable 04 — Foam Board
The Adjacency Matrix
The Adjacency Matrix format printed at 8.5″ × 11″, with every cell filled using the three-symbol key (● Primary, ○ Secondary, – No Relation), consistent with the Bubble Diagram and the floor plan. Pasted onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.
Deliverable 05 — Foam Board
The Client Profile
The completed Client Profile format: the selected client profile, the client backstory, and the client representation collage, made manually or digitally with real images (friends and family, magazines, or the internet; no AI-generated images). Printed and pasted onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.
Deliverable 06 — Format
The Design Concept Statement
The Design Concept Statement format with three paragraphs based on the client backstory: (1) feelings, atmospheric sensations, needs, and desires; (2) the activities in the main spaces of the house; (3) tangible design decisions on spatial configuration, materials, colors, textures, finishes, and lighting. All descriptions must match the floor plan and models from Assignment 05.
Deliverable 07 — Foam Board
The Material Board
The Material Board format with two boards, one for the bedrooms and one for the common areas, each with its DREAMS-4-MatBoard abstract bubble scheme, the step-by-step screenshots, and the material images created with Empathic Prompting and an AI image generator. Printed and pasted onto an 8.5″ × 11″ foam board base.
Deliverable 08 — Physical Assembly
The Synthesis Object
All 8.5″ × 11″ foam boards and the sliced 1/8″ model, connected in a unique and creative way with masking-tape hinges into a freestanding volumetric display. Panels are arranged in sequence from most abstract (Adjacency Matrix) to most concrete (sliced 1/8″ model). The structure must stand independently without support from a wall or other object.
Deliverable 09 — Process Documentation
Collaborative Memory Board
Continuous process documentation uploaded throughout all phases: measuring, drafting, cutting, building, and photographing. Entries must show genuine progression across the assignment, not only finalized work. Documentation uploaded only at submission in Blackboard will not meet this requirement.
